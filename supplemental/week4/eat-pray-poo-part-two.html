Supplemental Reading Week 4 Kratom Recall

Eat Pray Poo Part Two: Fecal Contamination in Kratom Products

Here we go again: another recall of so-called “herbal supplement” kratom powders for **Salmonella contamination**. The recall from Food and Drug Administration is a sharp reminder that products labelled as “natural” and “supplement” aren’t exempt from fecal-borne risks. [oai_citation:0‡U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/vanguard-enterprises-llc-dba-bedrock-mfg-recalls-monarch-premium-kratom-powder-because-possible?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The company Vanguard Enterprises LLC (dba Bedrock MFG) issued a recall on October 28, 2025 covering multiple lots of its Monarch Premium Kratom powders (Bali Gold, Red Bali, Green Maeng Da, White Elephant) due to potential Salmonella contamination. Also, note how it remained on the market for 2 years before being caught. [oai_citation:1‡U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/vanguard-enterprises-llc-dba-bedrock-mfg-recalls-monarch-premium-kratom-powder-because-possible?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Let’s call this exactly what it is: **fecal contamination**. Salmonella is typically spread via fecal matter from infected animals or people, or via cross-contamination in processing. The fact that an herb marketed as “wellness” could carry such a risk is not only alarming—it’s a disgrace.

When Upton Sinclair Meets the Herbal Aisle

We remember Upton Sinclair’s *The Jungle* and the 1906 expose of meat-packing horrors (“I aimed for the public’s heart and by accident I hit their stomach”). Today, look at the kratom aisle: powders bagged, shipped, marketed as safe—even while the risk of microbes, pests, and fecal spores remains unaddressed.

What the Science Shows

A recent peer-reviewed study found that certain kratom products contained high microbial loads, including Escherichia coli and Salmonella, reflecting serious gaps in manufacturing controls and plant-based supplement safety. When we talk about *supplements* we talk “no regulation, no oversight, no assured safety,” despite real risks of illness. Why are kratom powders being treated like vitamins when they carry the same microbial hazard as leafy greens, herbs and spices?

Why This Matters for Families

  • Children, the elderly, and people with weakened immune systems face higher risk of severe infections from Salmonella: bloodstream infection, arthritis, endocarditis. [oai_citation:3‡U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/vanguard-enterprises-llc-dba-bedrock-mfg-recalls-monarch-premium-kratom-powder-because-possible?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
  • Many kratom products are bought online or in stores with minimal oversight. This recall shows the vulnerabilities in the supply-chain.
  • Labeling “herbal” or “natural” creates a false sense of safety. This recall says: trust the label at your peril.

Actions for Parents & Policymakers

  1. Check any kratom product in your home: match the lot codes listed in the recall. If it appears on the recall list, **stop use immediately**. [oai_citation:4‡ConsumerLab.com](https://www.consumerlab.com/recalls/14939/monarch-premium-kratom-recalled-due-to-salmonella-risk/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
  2. Throw out or return any recalled product. Don’t assume “nothing has happened yet” means safe.
  3. Push for stronger laws: demand that kratom be subject to the same manufacturing controls as food (GMP, microbial testing, traceability). Why should a “supplement” be marching into store shelves with no oversight?
  4. Require penalties for companies that sell adulterated, contaminated, or mislabeled botanical products. Not “we’ll fix it later”—real consequences now.
  5. Educate your community: show them the recall. Bring up Sinclair’s words. Shine a light on the fact that “herbal high” powders can carry the same risk as contaminated produce.

Bottom Line

This recall is not some random fluke. It’s a symptom of a system that treats psychoactive botanical powders as “supplements” rather than potential public-health threats. The gap between marketing and safety is enormous. Families deserve better. Kids deserve better.
