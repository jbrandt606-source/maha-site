Amanita Muscaria: The Fairy-Tale Mushroom With a Dark Side

Why a red-and-white toadstool is being sold as a “legal high” in gummies and chocolate despite an FDA warning.

What is Amanita muscaria?

Amanita muscaria (fly-agaric) is the iconic red-capped mushroom seen in storybooks. It contains the psychoactive compounds muscimol and ibotenic acid, which act on GABA receptors in the brain. Unlike psilocybin mushrooms, Amanita’s effects are unpredictable — ranging from sedation and euphoria to vomiting, delirium, and loss of coordination. Potency varies by specimen and preparation, so “dose” is unreliable.

What the FDA said in 2024

FDA determination (2024): Amanita muscaria and its constituents (muscimol, ibotenic acid, muscarine) are not approved for use in food. Foods containing them are considered adulterated under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.

Parents’ translation: these ingredients haven’t been shown safe as food; the FDA has warned industry and the public to avoid mushroom edibles made with Amanita constituents.

How is this legal?

  • Not scheduled federally: Because Amanita doesn’t contain psilocybin, it falls outside federal drug schedules and is widely sold.
  • Only Louisiana bans sale for human consumption: Louisiana RS 40:989.1 explicitly prohibits Amanita muscaria for human consumption.
  • Most states have no age limits: In much of the U.S., Amanita products can be sold without age checks, potency testing, or labeling standards.

Even experts have been harmed

Renowned mycologist Paul Stamets described a severe Amanita experience on The Joe Rogan Experience — convulsions, blackout, and a 12-hour ordeal. His takeaway was simple: “Legal doesn’t mean safe.”

What parents and policymakers can do

  • Require age limits (21+) for Amanita-containing products.
  • Impose strict penalties for sales to minors and child-appealing marketing.
  • Mandate clear labeling, potency testing, and FDA-compliant oversight.
  • Consider a statewide sales ban until independent safety data exists.
