Amanita Muscaria — The Fairy-Tale Mushroom With a Dark Side

Why a red-and-white toadstool is being sold as a “legal high” in candy form — and why parents should be concerned.

What is Amanita muscaria?

Amanita muscaria (fly-agaric) is the familiar red-and-white mushroom from storybooks. It contains muscimol and ibotenic acid, which act on GABA receptors in the brain. Unlike psilocybin mushrooms, Amanita’s effects are unpredictable and can include heavy sedation, vomiting, delirium, and loss of coordination. Potency varies by specimen and preparation, so “dose” is unreliable.

What the FDA said (2024)

FDA determination: Amanita muscaria and its constituents (muscimol, ibotenic acid, muscarine) are not approved for use in food. Foods containing them are considered adulterated under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. The agency has warned industry and consumers to avoid mushroom edibles made with Amanita constituents.

How is this legal?

  • Not scheduled federally: Because Amanita doesn’t contain psilocybin, it falls outside federal drug schedules and is widely sold.
  • Only Louisiana bans sale for human consumption: Louisiana RS 40:989.1 prohibits Amanita muscaria products for human consumption.
  • Most states have no age limits: In much of the U.S., Amanita products are sold with no ID checks, potency testing, or labeling standards.

Even experts have been harmed

Renowned mycologist Paul Stamets described a severe Amanita experience on The Joe Rogan Experience — convulsions, blackout, and a 12-hour ordeal. His takeaway: “Legal doesn’t mean safe.”

What parents and policymakers can do

  1. Require age limits (21+) for Amanita-containing products.
  2. Impose strict penalties for sales to minors and child-appealing marketing.
  3. Mandate clear labeling, potency testing, and oversight.
  4. Consider a statewide sales ban until independent safety data exists.
